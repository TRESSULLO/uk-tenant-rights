Know Your Rights as a Tenant in the UK

Free, clear and original guidance for tenants in England and Wales — updated for the Renters' Rights Act 2025

Get Started

🏦 Deposits

Your landlord must protect your deposit in a government-approved scheme within 30 days of receiving it.

Approved Protection Schemes

In England and Wales, landlords must use one of three government-approved deposit protection services:

  • Deposit Protection Service (DPS)
  • MyDeposits
  • Tenancy Deposit Scheme (TDS)

Getting Your Deposit Back

Once you both agree on the final amount, your landlord has 10 days to return it. Deductions are only permitted for:

  • Unpaid rent
  • Damage beyond fair wear and tear
  • Items missing from the agreed inventory

Deposit Cap Rules

Since June 2019, deposit amounts have been legally capped:

  • Up to 5 weeks' rent for annual rent under £50,000
  • Up to 6 weeks' rent for annual rent of £50,000 or more

🔧 Repairs & Maintenance

Your landlord has a legal duty to keep your home safe, structurally sound and in good repair.

Landlord's Responsibilities

  • Structural repairs — walls, roof, windows, floors
  • Heating systems and hot water supply
  • Gas safety and electrical installations
  • Addressing damp or mould from structural causes
  • Dealing with pest infestations

How to Report a Repair

  • Always report in writing — email is ideal
  • Be specific: describe the problem clearly
  • Photograph or video the issue as evidence
  • Keep copies of all messages and responses
  • Note the date you first reported it

If Repairs Are Ignored

  • Send a formal written reminder with a deadline
  • Contact your local council's housing department
  • The council may inspect and enforce repairs
  • Seek independent legal or housing advice

🚪 Eviction

Your landlord must follow a strict legal process to end your tenancy. Since the Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into force on 1 May 2026, tenants have significantly stronger protections.

⚠️ Section 21 Has Been Abolished

  • As of 1 May 2026, Section 21 "no fault" evictions no longer exist
  • Your landlord cannot evict you simply because they want the property back
  • They must now have a specific legal reason to end your tenancy
  • Break clauses in older agreements can no longer be used to evict you
  • This applies to all private tenancies, new and existing

Section 8 Notice

  • Now the only route for landlords to seek possession
  • Must cite a specific legal ground — e.g. rent arrears, anti-social behaviour, or the landlord genuinely moving back in
  • Notice period varies depending on the ground used
  • A court order is still required before any eviction can take place
  • Seek advice immediately if you receive a Section 8 notice

Unlawful Eviction

  • Changing locks without a court order is illegal
  • Removing your belongings without consent is illegal
  • Harassment or intimidation is a criminal offence
  • Call 999 if you are physically removed from your home
  • You can also report unlawful eviction to your local council

📄 Tenancy Agreements

Your tenancy agreement is a legally binding contract. Since 1 May 2026, all private tenancies in England are now Assured Tenancies — always read your agreement in full before signing.

Your New Tenancy Type

  • Assured Shorthold Tenancies (ASTs) no longer exist from 1 May 2026
  • All private tenants now have Assured Tenancies (ATs)
  • Fixed-term tenancies have been abolished — all tenancies are now periodic (rolling)
  • You generally need to give your landlord 2 months' notice to leave
  • This gives you greater security and stronger protection from eviction

Rent Increases

  • Your landlord can only raise your rent once per year
  • They must give you at least 2 months' written notice using the correct form
  • You have the right to challenge an increase you believe is above market rate
  • Contact a housing adviser if you feel a proposed increase is unfair

Documents Your Landlord Must Provide

  • A signed copy of the tenancy agreement
  • A current Energy Performance Certificate (EPC)
  • A valid Gas Safety Record (if applicable)
  • The government's Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet (from May 2026)
  • Deposit protection confirmation and scheme details

New Rights Under the 2025 Act

  • Stronger right to request a pet — landlords cannot unreasonably refuse
  • Protection against discrimination if you have children or receive benefits
  • Access to a new Private Rental Sector Landlord Ombudsman
  • Landlords must meet the Decent Homes Standard
  • Awaab's Law applies — landlords must fix damp and mould within set timeframes

✉️ Letter Templates

These are original template letters. Replace all [bracketed] sections with your own details before sending.

[Your Full Name] [Your Address] [Town, Postcode] [Your Email Address] [Date] [Landlord's Full Name or Agency Name] [Landlord's Address] Dear [Landlord's Name], Re: Request for Repair — [Your Property Address] I am writing to formally notify you of a repair issue at the above property, which I have rented from you since [tenancy start date]. The problem I am experiencing is as follows: [Describe the issue clearly — e.g. "The boiler has not been producing hot water since [date]. I reported this verbally on [date] but the issue has not been resolved."] As my landlord, you have a legal duty under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1985 to keep the property in good repair. I would be grateful if you could arrange for this matter to be inspected and resolved within [14 / 21] days of this letter. Please confirm receipt of this letter and let me know your intended course of action. I am happy to provide access to the property at a mutually convenient time. If I do not hear from you within the above timeframe, I may need to seek further assistance, including contacting my local council's housing department. Yours sincerely, [Your Full Name] [Your Contact Number]
[Your Full Name] [Your Former Address] [Town, Postcode] [Your Email Address] [Date] [Landlord's Full Name or Agency Name] [Landlord's Address] Dear [Landlord's Name], Re: Deposit Dispute — [Former Property Address] I am writing regarding the deposit of £[amount] that I paid at the start of my tenancy, which began on [start date] and ended on [end date]. I have not yet received a full refund of my deposit, and I do not agree with the deductions proposed. Specifically, I dispute the following: [List each disputed deduction, e.g. "£150 for cleaning — the property was professionally cleaned on [date] and I have a receipt to confirm this."] Under the terms of the Tenancy Deposit Protection scheme, I understand that I am entitled to raise a formal dispute if we cannot agree. I am therefore requesting that this matter be referred to the deposit scheme's free adjudication service. Please respond within 14 days to confirm your position. I have retained all relevant evidence, including the original inventory, photographs taken at the start and end of the tenancy, and all written communications between us. I hope we can resolve this matter promptly and without the need for further action. Yours sincerely, [Your Full Name] [Your Contact Number]
[Your Full Name] [Your Address] [Town, Postcode] [Your Email Address] [Date] [Landlord's Full Name or Agency Name] [Landlord's Address] Dear [Landlord's Name], Re: Notice to Vacate — [Property Address] I am writing to formally give notice that I intend to end my tenancy at the above address. In accordance with my tenancy agreement, I am giving [number] weeks' / months' notice. My intended move-out date is therefore: [date]. I will ensure the property is left in a clean and tidy condition, matching the state described in the original inventory, and I will return all keys to you by [date]. I would also like to arrange a check-out inspection at a mutually convenient time before my departure. Please let me know your available dates. Could you also confirm the process for returning my deposit of £[amount] once the property has been checked and any agreed costs settled. Thank you for our tenancy arrangement. Please confirm receipt of this notice in writing. Yours sincerely, [Your Full Name] [Your Contact Number]
